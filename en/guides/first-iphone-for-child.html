Your child's first iPhone: a setup checklist

Updated 5 November 2026

Before you hand over your child's first iPhone, take 15 minutes to set it up: add your child to Family Sharing with a child account, turn on Ask to Buy, set content limits for their age, schedule Downtime for bedtime, and decide which apps you can lock. It's much easier to start with rules than to add them later.

1. Create a child account in Family Sharing

  1. On your iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Family.
  2. Tap Add Member and choose to create an account for a child. Use your child's real birth date: iOS sets age-appropriate defaults from it.
  3. Sign in to the new iPhone with your child's account, not yours, so their apps, photos and messages stay separate from yours.

2. Turn on Ask to Buy

In Settings → your name → Family → your child → Ask to Buy. Every app download and in-app purchase then needs your approval on your phone. This also stops surprise bills from games.

3. Set content limits for their age

Under Settings → Screen Time → your child → Content & Privacy Restrictions:

  • Set the App Store age rating to fit your child, for example 9+ or 12+.
  • Limit adult websites in Safari.
  • Under Communication Limits, choose who your child can call and message.

4. Plan bedtime and school hours

Use Downtime for the night, for example from an hour before bed until morning, and keep Phone and Messages in Always Allowed. Many families also agree that the phone charges outside the bedroom at night. Not sure how much screen time fits your child's age? Try our screen time planner.

5. Add a lock you can use any time

Schedules cover the routine, but family life isn't routine: dinner, homework, a sleepover that runs late. Blok: Parental Control lets you lock the apps you picked from your own iPhone in one tap, for 30 minutes, an hour, two hours or until you unlock. Install it on both phones while the new iPhone is still on your kitchen table.

6. Agree on the rules together

Tell your child what's set up and why, before the first argument. Rules your child helped write are easier to keep. Write down three or four, for example: no phone at dinner, phones charge in the kitchen at night, ask before you download, and tell a parent if something online feels wrong.

Questions

What age should a child get their first iPhone?

There's no single right age, and advice differs between countries. Many parents wait until around the start of secondary school. What matters most is that the phone comes with clear rules and settings that fit your child's age.

Should my child use my Apple Account on their iPhone?

No. Give your child their own child account in Family Sharing. Then Screen Time, Ask to Buy and content limits work, and your messages and photos stay on your phone only.

Can I set up parental controls before I give the phone as a present?

Yes. You can set up the child account, Screen Time and Blok before you wrap it. On the day, your child unlocks it with the passcode you set, and you change it together.
