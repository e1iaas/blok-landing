How much screen time by age?

Updated 5 November 2026

Sweden's Public Health Agency (Folkhälsomyndigheten) recommends at most 1 hour of screen time a day for children aged 2 to 5, 1 to 2 hours for ages 6 to 12, and 2 to 3 hours for teenagers aged 13 to 18. Schoolwork doesn't count. It also advises no screens just before bed and no phones in the bedroom at night.

Source: Folkhälsomyndigheten, 2024. Advice from other countries' health agencies is similar in spirit, although not all of them give exact numbers.

What counts as screen time?

The limits are about free time on screens: games, social media, videos and scrolling. Homework on a laptop, listening to music or a podcast, and reading e-books usually don't count. Neither does a video call with grandparents.

It also matters what fills the rest of the day. Sleep, school, moving and seeing friends come first; screen time fits in around them.

How to make the limits stick

  1. Make a plan for the week with your child, so the rules are known before anyone has to say no. Our screen time planner builds one from your child's age, school hours and bedtime.
  2. Lock school hours and bedtime on a schedule in Screen Time or Blok, so you don't have to start the conversation every evening.
  3. Lock on the spot when it's needed. For dinner or homework, one tap on your phone is easier than a negotiation.
  4. Keep Phone and Messages open so your child can always reach you.
  5. Be a role model. Children copy what adults do, so it helps to put your own phone away at dinner too.

Questions

How much screen time should a 10 year old have?

For children aged 6 to 12, Sweden's Public Health Agency recommends at most 1 to 2 hours of leisure screen time a day, with no screens just before bed. Schoolwork doesn't count towards it.

How much screen time is too much for a teenager?

For ages 13 to 18 the Swedish guidance is at most 2 to 3 hours of leisure screen time a day. More important than the exact number is that screens don't push out sleep, school, exercise and friends.

Does gaming count as screen time?

Yes. Games, social media, videos and scrolling all count as leisure screen time. Schoolwork, music, podcasts and e-books usually don't.
